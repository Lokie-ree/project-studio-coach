## Playtester notes

**Form vs conversation.** Spark and expand felt like an actual conversation — the coach picked up "sad girl hours," "the order matters," and "MY picks" and used them, which is exactly what makes a kid keep typing. It got form-shaped at two moments: (1) the "Can you say your first version as: 'I want to make something that...'" prompt — that's a worksheet blank, and Maya even apologized for filling it in badly ("idk how to say it better lol"); (2) the Milestone Zero formula ("By [day], I will have [output] so that I can [next thing]") followed by "Type the full sentence in your own words" — the coach had ALREADY said the M0 sounded right-sized, then made me re-type it in template form anyway. Asking a 15-year-old to restate a thing she just said, but in the official sentence shape, is peak form-mode.

**Milestone Zero.** Genuinely landed as a win, not an assignment — "8 songs written out with why i picked each one" came from Maya's own "the fun part" and she said "i could literally do that tonight tbh" unprompted. The under-two-hours framing worked. The only sour note: after committing, the coach re-asked "Can you finish it this week?" when Maya had already said "yeah thursday for sure" — asking for the same commitment twice feels like it wasn't listening.

**Reflexive praise / words in mouth.** Mostly restrained — quotes were verbatim, which felt respectful. But a few tics: "'kinda bad' still counts" is nice once; "That sounds the right size" + "I hear it" + "That sounds like the core" is a validation cadence you start to hear. One real words-in-mouth moment: "'cute' gives the design a job without requiring it to look 'super pro'" — Maya never said design had "a job," that's coach-speak wrapped around her word. Also the double question habit: the coach frequently asked two things at once ("what rule do you want... AND how would you pitch this") and a real teen answers one and drops the other.

**Menu mismatch.** The chat listed 7 shapes but the actual menu panel has 24, and the shape Maya actually picked (recommendation tool) was NOT in the chat's list of 7 — she picked it from the panel. If she had only read the chat she'd have never seen the best-fit archetype. The chat list also skewed weirdly (font specimen page? for a music kid?).

**The reveal.** Best moment of the session. Seeing a past student's plan for the same archetype AFTER committing to mine made me instantly compare and defend my choices ("mine has the jess test... i win imo"), and the coach's response — "That's a difference on purpose" — validated the divergence instead of grading it. That's the moment the plan felt owned rather than assigned.

**Would a real 15-year-old close the tab?** Two danger points: (1) the wall-of-text archetype list right after her first vulnerable attempt at a goal — seven bullet points with time estimates reads like homework; (2) being asked to re-type her M0 in formula form. Also the rate limit stalled the session mid-flow for real minutes — a kid does not wait 80 seconds between messages; she's on TikTok by second 20. Everything else — short replies, her words quoted back, the "annoying scope questions, and they're not a trap" framing — would keep her in the room.
