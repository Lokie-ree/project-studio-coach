# Scope Examples: Before → After Rewrites
How to take a big idea and trim it to something finishable in 1–2 weeks, without losing what makes it interesting.
 
Use these during the Narrow step when a student's idea is exciting but too large to execute.
 
---
 
## How to Use This File
 
When a student describes a project, ask yourself: **Is this a 1-month idea being attempted in 1 week?**
 
If yes, use the pattern below:
1. Name what's exciting about their big version (validate it)
2. Identify the one core mechanic or value
3. Cut everything that requires the full version to be useful
4. Define a "proof of concept" version that delivers that core value alone
---
 
## Scope Rewrites by Category
 
---
 
### Games
 
**Before:** "A multiplayer battle royale game where 100 players fight on a shrinking map with different weapons and abilities."
**After:** "A 2-player local turn-based battle game with 3 weapons and a 10x10 grid map — playable in the browser."
- What we kept: the combat core
- What we cut: multiplayer infrastructure, map shrinking, ability system, matchmaking
- Why it works: two people can actually play and enjoy it this week
**Before:** "An open-world RPG with quests, a crafting system, and a day/night cycle."
**After:** "A text adventure with 3 rooms, 2 items to find, and one puzzle to solve — with a hand-drawn map as a bonus visual."
- What we kept: exploration and discovery
- What we cut: graphics engine, quest trees, crafting, time systems
- Why it works: the fun of RPGs is problem-solving and story, not the engine
**Before:** "A rhythm game like Guitar Hero with custom songs."
**After:** "A rhythm game with one song and 3 difficulty levels — hit the key on the beat, get a score."
- What we kept: the core mechanic (timing + feedback)
- What we cut: song library, custom uploads, multiplayer, leaderboard
- Why it works: one song well-executed teaches everything
---
 
### Apps and Tools
 
**Before:** "A social media app where people share their art and follow each other."
**After:** "A personal gallery webpage — upload your own art, add a title and date, see it displayed in a clean grid."
- What we kept: showing work beautifully
- What we cut: accounts, followers, feeds, comments, moderation
- Why it works: a beautiful personal gallery is valuable and shippable; a social network is a company
**Before:** "An app that tracks your fitness, logs meals, shows calories, and gives workout recommendations."
**After:** "A single-purpose workout log: enter today's exercise and duration, see a 7-day history chart."
- What we kept: the habit and accountability loop
- What we cut: meal tracking, calorie database, AI recommendations, integrations
- Why it works: the log itself creates the behavior change — the rest is polish
**Before:** "A marketplace where people can buy and sell handmade goods."
**After:** "A single seller storefront page — 6 product cards with photos, prices, and a 'Contact to order' link."
- What we kept: showcasing products and inviting buyers
- What we cut: payments, listings management, buyer accounts, search, reviews
- Why it works: a DM-to-order model works at small scale and has zero payment infrastructure risk
**Before:** "A tutoring platform where teachers can post lessons and students can submit assignments."
**After:** "A single-subject study guide webpage — structured notes, 5 practice problems, and a self-check answer key."
- What we kept: the learning value
- What we cut: accounts, submission system, teacher dashboard, notifications
- Why it works: the content is the product; the platform is a v3 problem
---
 
### Data and Research
 
**Before:** "An analysis of every country's economic data since 1900 to find patterns."
**After:** "A data story comparing 5 countries' GDP growth over the last 30 years, with 3 charts and a 300-word interpretation."
- What we kept: the analytical insight and storytelling
- What we cut: 120 years of data, 195 countries, complex modeling
- Why it works: a focused comparison makes a stronger argument than a sprawling one
**Before:** "A live dashboard showing real-time stock prices, news sentiment, and trading signals."
**After:** "A static analysis of one company's stock price over the past year, with annotations for key events."
- What we kept: the financial insight
- What we cut: real-time data feeds, sentiment analysis APIs, trading logic
- Why it works: historical analysis teaches the same skills without the infrastructure
**Before:** "A survey of everyone at my school about their mental health, with statistics and recommendations."
**After:** "A short illustrated explainer — 'What stress actually does to your brain' — based on 3 published studies, shared as a PDF or web page."
- What we kept: the educational impact on the audience
- What we cut: data collection from real students, privacy considerations, statistical analysis
- Why it works: you don't need original data to make something meaningful and shareable
---
 
### Creative and Design
 
**Before:** "An animated short film with original characters, voice acting, and a full story arc."
**After:** "A 60-second animated loop — one character, one environment, one repeated action — with original music or sound."
- What we kept: the animation craft and atmosphere
- What we cut: story arc, multiple characters, voice acting, editing
- Why it works: a polished loop teaches the same skills as a 10-minute film
**Before:** "A magazine with articles, photo essays, interviews, and a design system."
**After:** "A 4-page zine: one essay (400 words), two images, one pull quote, and a cover — exported as a PDF."
- What we kept: editorial voice and visual design
- What we cut: volume of content, ongoing publishing schedule, team
- Why it works: one great issue > ten mediocre ones
**Before:** "A font I designed from scratch with a full character set and multiple weights."
**After:** "A display font with just uppercase letters (A–Z), displayed on a specimen page with 3 example uses."
- What we kept: the design process and the artifact
- What we cut: lowercase, numbers, punctuation, italics, weights, OpenType features
- Why it works: 26 characters done well is a real deliverable; 200+ is a career
---
 
### Community and Advocacy
 
**Before:** "An app that connects volunteers with local nonprofits and tracks hours."
**After:** "A single-page directory of 10–15 local volunteer opportunities with descriptions, links, and contact info — updated manually."
- What we kept: connecting people to opportunities
- What we cut: accounts, matching algorithm, hour tracking, nonprofit onboarding
- Why it works: a curated list people can actually find and use is more valuable than a half-built platform
**Before:** "A petition site where anyone can create and sign petitions on any topic."
**After:** "One petition page for one specific issue — with a clear ask, a short explanation, and a Google Form to collect signatures."
- What we kept: mobilizing people around an issue
- What we cut: user-generated petitions, authentication, spam prevention, moderation
- Why it works: the issue you care about is the project; the platform is a distraction

## The Scope Rewrite Formula
 
When helping a student scope down, use this structure:
 
> "What I love about your idea is [the core value]. The version that takes a month to build adds [feature A, B, C] on top of that core. But you can get all the way to [core value] by just building [minimal version]. Here's what that looks like: [one-sentence description]. What do you think — does that still feel exciting?"
 
---
 
## Red Flags That Signal a Scope Problem
 
- The idea requires other people to sign up or create accounts to be useful
- The idea requires real-time data from an external service
- The idea has more than one "main" user (e.g., both students AND teachers)
- The student describes it as "like [major app] but..."
- The student hasn't mentioned what version 1 looks like — only the final vision
When you spot these, ask: **"What's the smallest version of this that you'd actually be proud to show someone?"**
